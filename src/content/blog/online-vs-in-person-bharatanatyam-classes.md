We teach both — at our studio in Vastral, Ahmedabad, and live online for families in the USA and Canada — so we have no reason to oversell either. Here is how they honestly compare.

## What a studio does best

- **Hands-on correction.** A teacher in the room can adjust a knee or an elbow directly, which is the fastest way for a young child to feel the right position.
- **Dancing to one beat.** Everyone moves to the same rhythm at the same moment. Over video, that is impossible — more on that below.
- **Space and floor.** Room to travel across the floor, on a surface suited to footwork.
- **Fewer distractions.** No screens, no siblings wandering past the camera.

## What online does well

- **Access to the right teacher.** Your child can learn from a teacher of a specific lineage, however far away you live.
- **Individual correction, if the batch is small.** With six to eight children, a teacher can watch every video tile and correct each child by name.
- **Time that fits the family.** No commute, and classes can be scheduled around school weeks.
- **Recordings to revisit.** When classes are recorded, a missed session or a tricky sequence can be caught up at home.

## What online can't do — honestly

Video has limits, and a good online teacher designs around them rather than pretending they are not there.

- **Lag.** Sound and picture always arrive slightly late, so a teacher and students can never dance perfectly together to one count. Online classes work better when the teacher demonstrates, then watches and corrects.
- **Flat pictures.** One camera loses depth, and feet slip out of frame easily. A teacher can only correct what she can see.
- **The floor at home.** Bharatanatyam is danced barefoot with strong footwork, and slippery tiles or thick carpet both get in the way.
- **A parent's help.** Younger children need someone to set up the camera and stay nearby.

## Setting up for an online class

A little preparation makes an online class far more useful:

1. **Clear a floor space** of about two metres by two metres.
2. **Place the laptop or tablet** a few steps back, in landscape, so your child's whole body is visible — feet included.
3. **Light from the front.** A window behind your child turns her into a silhouette.
4. **Mind the floor.** Wood or a firm, thin mat is kinder than bare tile for daily footwork.
5. **Clothes that show the line of the legs,** so the teacher can see the knees in aramandi.

## So which should you choose?

If a teacher from the lineage you want teaches small batches near you, a studio is hard to beat. If not, a small live online batch with the right teacher is usually a better choice than a large local class or a pre-recorded video course. Correction is how this dance is learned — choose the option where your child will get the most of it.
